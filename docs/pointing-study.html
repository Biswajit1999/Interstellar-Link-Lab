Sometimes the photons arrive. The message doesn't.

The direction of a laser beam can drift during a packet. When that happens, average signal power can tell a very misleading story about how often the entire packet survives.

This model tests a Gaussian laser mode over 4.24 light-years with 8-PPM and ideal photon-count detection. Change the pointing jitter and watch the whole-message reliability.

Why the two curves disagree

Imagine taking an entire photograph while the telescope is slightly mispointed. Some packets are transmitted in good alignment, but others experience a continuous period of severe light loss. Replacing that variation with one average photon rate disguises the missing packets.

Mean gain = 1 / (1 + 4 × (range × jitter / beam radius)²)
Whole packet success = average over pointing [ (1 − (1−1/M) × exp(−s₀ × gain))ⁿ ]

Every plotted probability refers to all 16 raw PPM symbols being correct. There is no forward error correction, beacon acquisition, physical frame synchronisation, detector dead time or background model in this calculation.

Different beam models. This example uses a Gaussian TEM00 mode. The earlier 0.363-photons/s example used a uniformly illuminated circular aperture (Airy pattern). Those are not the same transmitter pupil, so their baseline rates are not directly interchangeable.
